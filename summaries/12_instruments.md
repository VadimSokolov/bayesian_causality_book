Instruments identify effects under unmeasured confounding at the price of an exclusion restriction, and this chapter develops the Bayesian treatment. It starts from the linear model and the local average treatment effect as an accounting over compliers, then builds Bayesian instrumental variables. With one weak instrument a posterior reports the weakness as a wide interval, whether the prior sits on the reduced form or on the structural model. With twenty weak instruments a diffuse structural prior is confident and wrong, and a shrunk first stage gives intervals that cover the truth by being wide. A horseshoe on the first stage handles many instruments. The chapter also treats unlikely instruments, kernel methods for nonlinear structural functions, with a proposed global local prior, and Mendelian randomization, which turns Fisher's genetic confounder into an instrument.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on instrumental variables (Wright 1928), the local average treatment effect (Imbens and Angrist 1994; Angrist, Imbens and Rubin 1996), the returns to schooling application (Card 1995), weak instruments (Bound, Jaeger and Baker 1995; Staiger and Stock 1997), Anderson Rubin sets (Anderson and Rubin 1949), Mendelian randomization (Davey Smith and Ebrahim 2003).
