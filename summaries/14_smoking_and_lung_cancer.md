Fisher doubted that smoking causes lung cancer, and this chapter asks how a Bayesian should weigh the evidence. Fisher was right that observation cannot exclude every common cause and wrong about which way the evidence pointed. Cornfield's inequality, dose response, the twin studies, the inhalation paradox, the front door criterion and Mendelian randomization each constrain the alternatives, and the chapter says what a Bayesian synthesis would combine, with the assumptions in view. Bradford Hill's considerations are read as prior information. The front door section simulates a population with a hidden genotype, and a computed illustration, with no cohort data, gives the posterior of the effect under stated priors on the strength of a hidden confounder. The chapter is a historical case study as much as a method, and it says plainly where the data cannot decide.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on Fisher's constitutional hypothesis (Fisher 1958), Cornfield's inequality (Cornfield and coauthors 1959), Bradford Hill's considerations (Hill 1965), the front door adjustment (Pearl 1995), Mendelian randomization (Davey Smith and Ebrahim 2003).
