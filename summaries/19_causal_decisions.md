A causal decision maximizes expected utility under an intervention, and the posterior does the averaging, so the Bayes action depends on the whole posterior and not only on the average effect. This chapter separates evidential from causal expected utility and treats policy evaluation, structural estimation and the Lucas critique, games and strategic interaction, industrial organization, mechanism design, targeting and cost effectiveness, climate and fiscal policy, and behavioural departures. A result bounds the regret of the Bayes policy by the posterior's expected error. The chapter ends with risk sensitive decisions computed from a posterior. The chapter is mostly a map of where posterior based decisions differ from decisions based on one estimate, with worked examples and exercises.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on expected utility (von Neumann and Morgenstern 1944), causal decision theory (Lewis 1981; Joyce 1999), the Lucas critique (Lucas 1976), generative Bayesian computation for maximum expected utility (Polson, Ruggeri and Sokolov 2024), distortion risk measures (Wang 2000).
