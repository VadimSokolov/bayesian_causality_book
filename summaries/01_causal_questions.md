Causal questions are questions about outcomes that were not observed, and this chapter sets up the Bayesian way to answer them. Potential outcomes name what would happen under each treatment, de Finetti's representation theorem turns a judgement of exchangeability into a model with a prior, and ignorability says which part of the model the data can inform. The causal targets are then functionals of one posterior: the average treatment effect, effects for subgroups and quantiles, and individual attributions. The chapter works one example from the posterior predictive imputation of the missing potential outcomes to the decision it supports, separates prediction from decision and policy, and flags interference between units as a limit of the framework. It proves no new theorem; its role is to fix the vocabulary and the logic that the remaining chapters use.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on the potential outcomes framework (Neyman 1923; Rubin 1974), exchangeability and the representation theorem (de Finetti 1937), ignorability and the propensity score (Rosenbaum and Rubin 1983), Bayesian inference for causal effects (Rubin 1978).
