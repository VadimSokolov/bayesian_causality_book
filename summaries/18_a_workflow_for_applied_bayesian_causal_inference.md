This chapter collects the book's advice into a seven step workflow for applied Bayesian causal inference: state the question and the population, draw the assignment mechanism, check overlap before modelling, fit at least two flexible outcome models, check the model against the data, report accountings and not a single number, and quantify sensitivity to unmeasured confounding. The first two steps ask for the same statement and the same graph under any method. In the later steps the Bayesian analysis uses its posterior for more than an average. A summary table reprints results from earlier chapters, and a closing section compares the workflow with DML and states the conditions under which, for one average effect, DML is enough.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on Bayesian workflow (Gelman and coauthors 2020), posterior predictive checks (Rubin 1984; Gelman, Meng and Stern 1996), overlap diagnostics (Crump and coauthors 2009; Li, Morgan and Zaslavsky 2018; Hill and Su 2013), sensitivity analysis (Cornfield and coauthors 1959; Rosenbaum 2002).
