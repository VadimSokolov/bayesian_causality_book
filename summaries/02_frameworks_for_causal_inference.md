This chapter surveys the frameworks of causal inference and shows how they relate. Fisher grounds inference in the randomization of a design, Neyman and Rubin in potential outcomes and an assignment mechanism, Pearl in structural causal models and the do calculus, Robins in nonparametric structural models and efficiency, and Dawid in decision theory without counterfactuals. The econometric tradition adds instruments, differences in differences and structural models. The chapter compares what each framework calls a cause and where each gets identification, then collects the shared estimation toolkit: outcome regression, weighting, doubly robust scores and learners for heterogeneous effects. It also gives overviews of the g methods for time varying treatments, causal discovery and transportability. A reader who wants only the Bayesian development can skip it on a first reading.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on randomization inference (Fisher 1935), potential outcomes (Neyman 1923; Rubin 1974), structural causal models and the do calculus (Pearl 1995, 2009), the g formula (Robins 1986), decision theoretic causality (Dawid 2015, 2021), the econometric tradition (Haavelmo 1943; Heckman and Vytlacil 2007; Imbens and Angrist 1994).
