This appendix collects the mathematical background that the chapters use without proof. It fixes conventions, then reviews conditioning and independence, Bayesian inference, large sample tools, linear regression and partialling out, and the distributional tools used with shrinkage priors. It states the Frisch, Waugh and Lovell theorem, which turns the coefficient of the treatment in a multiple regression into a regression of residuals, and closes with graphs, the do calculus and linear programs, which the bounds of Chapter 13 need. Nothing in it is new; it is there so that a reader with a first course in mathematical statistics can follow the proofs of the appendices that follow.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on the Frisch, Waugh and Lovell theorem (Frisch and Waugh 1933; Lovell 1963), the do calculus (Pearl 2009), large sample theory (van der Vaart 1998).
