# Chapter 7. Orthogonality Is Not Enough

Orthogonality removes the first order effect of a nuisance error, and this chapter asks what it leaves. The remainder is a signed difference of two channels, which the standard rate condition bounds in absolute value and so cannot see cancel. The chapter states a sharp rate condition, shows when the bound is attained under retained lasso shrinkage, and shows that if the cross channel were negligible beside the own square channel, a hypothesis its measurements do not support, the treatment error would attenuate the estimate toward zero. The reported interval comes from one nuisance fit and omits how the bias varies across fits, and no learner gives an interval of finite expected length that is valid uniformly in overlap. The same anatomy appears in weighting. A posterior over the nuisance pair integrates over the fit, but a Bayesian analysis with the prior on the structural coefficients does far worse than DML. The evidence is mostly simulated, in linear Gaussian designs, with the 401(k), LaLonde and IHDP data as checks.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on orthogonal scores and cross fitting (Chernozhukov and coauthors 2018), regularization induced confounding (Hahn, Carvalho, Puelz and He 2018), impossibility of finite length uniform intervals under weak identification (Dufour 1997), the lasso and its rates (Belloni, Chernozhukov and Hansen 2014).

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Sharp rate condition](../RESULTS.md#sharp_rate_condition_for_dml) (Theorem 7.1, page 139; proved): The partialling out estimator with cross fitting is asymptotically normal when the root n times the bias term b, built from the two fitting errors, vanishes, under fourth moment conditions on the fitting errors. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026b), Orthogonality is not enough: double machine learning and the Bayesian alternative, working paper.
- [Alignment under retained shrinkage](../RESULTS.md#alignment_of_errors_under_retained_lasso_shrinkage) (Proposition 7.2, page 140; proved): When both regressions are fitted by the lasso with a common penalty and the shrunken fits are kept, the two fitting errors align in the limit, and the cross channel and the squared treatment error are each of the order of the penalty squared times the sparsity. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026b), Orthogonality is not enough: double machine learning and the Bayesian alternative, working paper.
- [Attenuation representation](../RESULTS.md#attenuation_representation) (Theorem 7.5, page 142; conditional on The hypothesis that the cross channel H_n is negligible relative to the own square channel Q_n is assumed, and the book does not show that any fitting method delivers it. The book's own measurements find the ratio not negligible): When the treatment fitting error does not vanish fast enough and the cross channel is negligible, the estimator is the true effect multiplied by a shrinkage factor below one. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026b), Orthogonality is not enough: double machine learning and the Bayesian alternative, working paper.
- [Cross fitting: the identity that survives](../RESULTS.md#cross_fitted_variance_identity) (Proposition 7.7, page 146; proved): The variance of the cross fitted estimator splits exactly into the variance of the bias across random fits, a covariance and the variance of the remainder, so an interval that ignores the first term can undercover. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026b), Orthogonality is not enough: double machine learning and the Bayesian alternative, working paper.
- [No uniformly valid interval of finite length](../RESULTS.md#no_uniformly_valid_interval_of_finite_length) (Proposition 7.8, page 149; proved): For a fixed sample size, no confidence set for the partialling out effect can both have uniform coverage over all treatment variances and have expected length bounded over that class; the proof follows Gleser and Hwang (1987) and Dufour (1997). First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026b), Orthogonality is not enough: double machine learning and the Bayesian alternative, working paper.
- [Complete shrinkage bias](../RESULTS.md#complete_shrinkage_bias) (Proposition 7.10, page 152; proved): Shrinking the confounder coefficients entirely to zero and regressing on treatment alone leaves a limiting bias, the omitted variable bias, that grows toward one as the number of confounders grows when they enter both equations with equal coefficients. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026b), Orthogonality is not enough: double machine learning and the Bayesian alternative, working paper.
