This chapter lists twenty problems that earlier chapters raise and leave open. Each is stated as a setting, what is known, and a conjecture or an open question. They include a Bernstein von Mises theorem for the coupled horseshoe, coupling for nonlinear nuisances, the Robins and Ritov problem with an unknown propensity, default priors for couplings, rates for the triangular network posterior, calibration of amortized posteriors and of distorted values, and a course built on the book. Simulations show what to expect: the coupled horseshoe posterior reaches the efficient standard deviation by 1,600 observations, the coupled prior with an estimated propensity fails in the Robins and Ritov design, and a Student t working model halves the error of DML in a design with infinite variance errors. Seventeen of the problems ask for a theorem.

## From the literature

The chapter presents or builds on semiparametric Bernstein von Mises theorems (Castillo and Rousseau 2015), the Robins and Ritov example (Robins and Ritov 1997), posterior contraction for the horseshoe (van der Pas, Kleijn and van der Vaart 2014).
