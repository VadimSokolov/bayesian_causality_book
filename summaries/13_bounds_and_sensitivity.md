The probabilities that a treatment was necessary, sufficient, or both depend on the joint law of the potential outcomes, so they are bounded rather than identified. This chapter derives the bounds, shows how combining experimental and observational data narrows them, and gives the Bayesian reading: a posterior over the position inside the bounds gives a court the probability it actually needs. Partially identified posteriors keep a prior dependence that never washes out. The second half turns to unmeasured confounding. Cornfield's inequality, Rosenbaum's sensitivity parameter and the E value say how strong a hidden confounder must be to overturn a result, and a prior on the confounding parameters turns the threshold into a posterior probability that the effect is positive. Benchmarking on the 401(k) data and Lanarkshire numbers illustrate the method.

## From the literature
